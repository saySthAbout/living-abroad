검증일: 2026-07-29

---

If you want to immigrate to Canada permanently, including through Express Entry, you may be able to include your dependent children on your application. Children qualify as dependants if they are under 22 years old and do not have a spouse or common-law partner.

Children 22 years old or older (known as overage dependent children) can still qualify as dependants if they have depended on their parents for financial support since before they turned 22, and cannot financially support themselves because of a mental or physical condition.

Whether a child meets these requirements is assessed as of the "age lock-in date" — the date on which IRCC freezes the child's age for eligibility purposes, so the child cannot become ineligible simply because processing takes a long time. For most economic-class programs including Express Entry, the age lock-in date is the date IRCC receives the complete application for permanent residence. A child must still not be a spouse or common-law partner at that point, but passing the age limit afterward during processing does not disqualify them.

These rules apply specifically to permanent immigration categories including the economic classes (such as Express Entry); other categories like family class sponsorship, humanitarian and compassionate consideration, or protected persons may use different lock-in timing. Applicants should confirm their program's specific lock-in date before assuming a child's eligibility.
